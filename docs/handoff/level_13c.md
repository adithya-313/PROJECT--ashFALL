## Decisions and why
- Sourced Diurnal Hold Latency Formula (Amendment A-029): Hold response times are computed via `(base * bank_hold_delay_factor * time_of_day * weekend) + unreachable_extra_delay`. Night hours (22:00-06:00 IST) apply a 2.0x multiplier, weekends apply a 1.5x multiplier, and unreachable 1930 reporting draws add an extra delay. Reuses existing `bank.hold_delay_factor` from `world.banks` to eliminate duplicate delay parameterization (Issue 13-16).
- Three Hold Outcomes Only: Exhaustive state space is strictly partitioned into `applied`, `too_late`, and `missed`. If available balance is zero or negative when the hold lands, it is classified as `too_late` with `held = 0` and `shortfall = requested`. If funds are positive but less than requested, it is classified as `applied` with `held = balance` and `shortfall = requested - held`. If bank failed to act, outcome is `missed`.
- Two-Pass Replay via EncumbranceBook: Pass 1 generates baseline transactions and victim complaints. Stochastic latencies and bank response factors determine landing minutes. Eligible holds are converted into `OutsideEvent(kind='lien')` and injected into Pass 2. Pass 2 runs with identical seeds, reconstructs point-in-time balances from double-entry ledger history, and encumbers funds in the bank's `EncumbranceBook`.
- Naive One-Hop Forwarding (Issue 13-3): When a hold arrives partially or too late, the receiving bank inspects its own ledger for outgoing transfers that carried funds away before the landing minute. Exactly one forwarded hold directive is issued to the recipient bank. Further hops are strictly refused (`second_hop is None`) to avoid cyclical cascades or duplicate tracing in mock banks.
- Transactional National Outbox and Bank Isolation (Amendment A-030): National event publication uses a dedicated `national_outbox` PostgreSQL table written atomically within the bank transaction, keyed by bank ID (`f"{run_id}:{bank_id}"`). The relay forwards events to Kafka topics `registry.events` and `hold.requests`. Consumer helpers filter strictly on the bank's own messages, ensuring tenant isolation without full multi-tenant Postgres ACLs.
- Raw Identifier Redaction and Multi-Target Audit (Issue 13-2): Unhashed account IDs (`acc_...`), device fingerprints (`fp_...`), and phone numbers (`sim-...`) are replaced with `[redacted:<type>]` in structlog and OpenTelemetry trace attributes. Payloads crossing national boundaries carry HMAC `account_ref` instead of raw accounts. `core.raw_identifier_scan` audits five planes (nationalsim store export, Postgres outbox, Kafka topics, logs/Loki, and Tempo traces) and reports `NOT CHECKED` if any dependency is unreachable, never reporting false zero hits.
- Resilient Bank Outage Tracking: When nationalsim is unreachable, bank payment processing does not crash. `NationalsimBankClient` records outage intervals (`started_at`, `ended_at`, `calls_failed`), closes the outage upon recovery, and returns `status='unavailable', match=None` (never `match=False`).

## Known gaps and future work
- The two-pass limitation (A-029): Pass 1 establishes victim complaints and landing minutes, while Pass 2 injects primary holds as liens into the `EncumbranceBook`. Forwarded holds emitted by Pass 2 are evaluated against Pass 2 ledger balances but cannot place physical liens without a third simulation pass. This two-pass boundary is frozen by design.
- Forwarded holds not placed as liens: As specified in Amendment A-029, single-hop forwarded holds provide outcome classifications and traceback metrics at the second hop, but do not encumber recipient balances in the encumbrance overlay.
- The ACL limitation and the 0005/0006 change (A-030): `national_outbox` achieves bank isolation via application-level composite keying (`run_id:bank_id`) and topic partitioning rather than database row-level security or per-bank database users. In Level 14, migration 0006 will partition tables and introduce formal multi-tenant schema scoping.
- The `.env` vs container password mismatch on the owner's laptop (owner action): The owner's local `.env` contains local secrets that differ from default container credentials (`postgres:postgres`). CI and local test suites pass `$env:TEST_DATABASE_URL="postgresql://postgres:postgres@localhost:5432/ashfall"`. The repository `.env` is strictly preserved without modification; reconciling `.env` with running container secrets is an owner action.
